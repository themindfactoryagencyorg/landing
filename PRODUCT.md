# TheMindFactory landing

## Register

brand

## Users

Business owners and operations teams evaluating a partner to build software around their existing work. The first visit should explain the offer, show relevant work and make contacting the team easy.

## Product Purpose

Present custom applications, integrations and automation. Give the AI agent a prominent place as a main product, while making the wider custom development offer equally clear. Use the supplied David presentation as the source for the three client examples.

## Brand Personality

Human, practical, inventive. Preserve the existing mint identity, B1 logo, local fonts, neural scene and flowing light.

## Anti-references

Abstract architecture before a clear business benefit; generic AI promises; invented statistics; a website centered on two sales contacts instead of the full team.

## Design Principles

- Lead with the visitor's work and the problem they recognise.
- Explain applications, integrations and automation before the three real projects; highlight the AI agent after those projects.
- Show existing solutions before explaining internal product names.
- Present illustrative agent interactions explicitly as examples.
- Explain what happens after the first conversation.
- Preserve all seven team profiles and both languages.
- Keep one short process roadmap in the contact section. Training, launch,
  hosting and maintenance remain explicit. The internal MindHub/Minds explorer
  is omitted: offer, cases and the agent already explain the customer value.
  Team LinkedIn links use accessible icons beside names.
- Keep claims specific to the supplied examples. Avoid blanket promises such as
  “the system does the rest”. Explain the team's roles in concise, concrete bios.
- Put the contact action and email before the roadmap. A visitor ready to talk
  should not have to read every step first.
- On phones, open screenshots at a readable scale filling the viewing area,
  with native panning, a fixed close button and an optional full view. Desktop
  starts with the full view and offers actual-size detail with mouse dragging.
  Use a magnifier icon, not an external-link arrow. Touch gestures on the landing
  scroll instead of zooming; leave browser and OS accessibility zoom controls intact.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Keep the existing keyboard, language, reduced motion and no-JavaScript fallbacks. Use native disclosures for optional detail and readable contrast in both dark and light sections.
Dim the moving background behind reading sections. Mobile navigation must remain
usable without scripts, while enhanced navigation manages focus and page inertness.
